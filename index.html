Welcome

“Steamfunk” was coined by novelist Balogun Ojetade in 2012 as a philosophical/aesthetic critique of the ahistorical (and therefore inherently racist, sexism, classist, colonialist, and imperialist) steampunk movement which honors the Victorian era without reconning with any of the destructive, violent baggage it comes along with. STEAM also refers to the agronomy for science, technology, engineering, art, and mathematics. STEAMfrank, which incorporates a nod to owner Dr. Synatra Smith’s family nickname (Synatra = Sinatra > Frank), provides not only an opportunity to engage with contextualizing research and integrating immersive technologies to provide new ways to explore visual media. It also provides a peek under the proverbial hood to understand the inner workings of extended reality (XR) projects, including key questions to understand the way digital tools can enhance digitized and born digital media and considerations for greater discoverability and accessibility.

About the Project

Under the leadership of CLIR Program Officer Sharon M. Burney, CLIR has undertaken the “Safe Spaces for Cultural Heritage” initiative. The goal of this project is to create a network where Black librarians can get together, exchange stories, and help one another without worrying about retaliation. Burney’s aim is to promote healing, empowerment, and retention, acting as a catalyst for the development of equitable programs in local communities.

The program is also centered on addressing job-related trauma and establishing a safe space for healing in response to recent studies revealing the detrimental effects of such stress on BIPOC library employees. To move Black librarians from isolated experiences of trauma and attrition toward communal networking, empowerment, and retention, it is intended to create a more diverse, healthy, and empowered library workforce.

This program launched in 2024 in Charleston, South Carolina and brought together twenty Black librarians, five facilitators, and a digital journalist to record the proceedings. The workshop, which was held in a site of cultural and historical significance, promoted healing through the practice of “Sankofa,” or looking back at the past for meaning in the present.

All participants, myself included, received funding for a microgrant project that can be shared with all project participants in subsequent program years. After participating in the Podcasting from Scratch workshop through the Digital Humanities Summer Institute this past June, held at the University of Montreal, I developed a project entitled “Operation: Black Joy.” “Operation: Black Joy” is a multimedia podcast series for Black women working within and adjacent to the academy. The project is formatted to mimic the structure of a cassette tape, harkening back to the nostalgia of my childhood in the 1990s before life got complicated. This project is an extension of a Philadelphia Area Consortium of Special Collections Libraries (PACSCL) initiative entitled “Black Joy & Resilience,” the product of a planning grant received from the National Historical Publications & Records Commission to develop and test a collaborative digital edition that documents and illustrates Black Joy & Resilience in the greater Philadelphia area.

Within the site, users can navigate between Side A: Joy and Side B: Resilience to access “tracks” dedicated to the Black Joy & Resilience subdomains: play, growth, community. Each track includes a short podcast episode along with various resources related to the themes within each track. There is also a virtual scrapbook that allows users to select and upload an archival record from the Black Joy & Resilience digital edition along with the link to the item and a short personal affirmation. The selected item is added to the virtual scrapbook with a QR code overlaid in the bottom right corner as the front of a postcard. When scanned, webpage visitors can view the back of the postcard in AR with the message, a link to view the original item, and a stamp designed uniquely for programs where this project is presented or a standard stamp in cases where someone found the project outside of a specific program.
